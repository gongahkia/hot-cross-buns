Hot Cross Buns

Privacy and trust

What Hot Cross Buns stores, requests, and does not send.

Hot Cross Buns is a macOS desktop app that talks directly to Google Tasks and Google Calendar. There is no Hot Cross Buns-operated backend.

Google access

Hot Cross Buns asks Google only for the scopes it needs to read and write your selected task lists and calendars:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/tasks
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.metadata.readonly

The Tasks scope lets the app list task lists, read tasks, create tasks, update task titles, notes, due dates, completion state, hierarchy, and delete tasks or task lists when you choose those actions. The Calendar scope lets the app list calendars, read events, create events, update event details, reminders, recurrence, attendees, location, Google Meet links, and delete events when you choose those actions. Drive metadata access is used only for the user-initiated attachment picker; it does not read file contents. Hot Cross Buns does not request Gmail, Contacts, or Photos access.

How Google data is used

Google user data is used only to provide visible task, note, calendar, search, reminder, attachment-picker, and sync features. The app sends edits directly to Google Tasks and Google Calendar for the connected account, reads responses into the local cache, and uses local copies for fast search, offline display, pending offline writes, and diagnostics.

Hot Cross Buns does not sell Google user data, use it for advertising, transfer it to data brokers, or use it to train AI models.

Where local data lives

By default the app keeps a local cache under ~/Library/Application Support/Hot Cross Buns/ on macOS.

  • hot-cross-buns.sqlite: account metadata, settings, sync checkpoints, pending offline mutations, task/calendar mirrors, search indexes, and reminder state
  • local logs and diagnostics only when you choose to create or share them

The cache is replaceable. Google remains the source of truth for synced data.

Encryption and credentials

Google OAuth tokens are stored in macOS Keychain or, on the supported Fedora 43 KDE/Wayland RPM, KDE Wallet's Secret Service bridge; they are never stored in SQLite. The user-supplied Desktop OAuth client ID is stored separately in local settings; a Desktop client ID is not treated as a credential. Windows credential adapters remain deferred.

Sharing and retention

There is no Hot Cross Buns-operated server, hosted database, or analytics backend receiving your Google Tasks or Google Calendar data. Calendar events may also be visible to event attendees or calendar subscribers according to Google Calendar settings.

Disconnecting removes locally stored credentials for that account. You can revoke access from your Google Account. Data stored in Google remains there unless you delete it through Google or Hot Cross Buns.

Crash logs and telemetry

Hot Cross Buns does not include a third-party analytics or crash-upload SDK. The operating system may still produce local system crash reports, and the app may write a local crash note next to the cache to help diagnose launch failures. Those files stay on-device unless you choose to share them.

Notes, tasks, and events

Tasks and calendar events are backed by Google Tasks and Google Calendar. Notes are task-shaped items: the note body is stored in the Google Task notes field and the due date is empty.

Permissions

Notifications are optional and used only for device-local reminders derived from Google Calendar popup reminders. Email reminders stay in Google and do not create macOS notifications. You can revoke Google account access from Google account settings and disconnect Hot Cross Buns in Settings.

OAuth client ownership

Hot Cross Buns uses a Google Cloud Desktop OAuth client controlled by the person or organization using the app. The macOS app stores the user-supplied client ID in local settings and uses a localhost browser redirect. Do not use another person's OAuth client unless you trust that build and consent screen.

Release integrity

Verify a macOS artifact checksum before running it. Signing and notarization status must be established per release; do not infer it from this page.

Distribution model

Builds are distributed outside the App Store. They may be unsigned or not notarized; verify each release's status before installation. There is no automatic in-place updater.
